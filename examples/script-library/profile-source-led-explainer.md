## Speaking structure to adapt

- Start with the concrete incident and consequence, then supply context. Keep the opening intelligible before introducing a show name.
- Explain one event at a time using short spoken clauses. Use explicit transitions when changing story or returning to a previous detail.
- Attribute claims before describing them. Distinguish a complainant's account, a reported official action, and the presenter's interpretation.
- Place a short reaction after the specific detail that caused it, then continue the explanation. Let sentence length vary naturally.
- Give uncertainty and missing responses their own clear sentence. A reaction must not convert an allegation into an established fact.
- For multiple speakers, let one carry the chronology while others ask a concrete follow-up or challenge an inference. Avoid making every speaker repeat the same facts. Solo episodes should use direct narration without fabricated dialogue.
- Treat Malaysian particles and English words as optional natural speech. Use only where appropriate to the configured host and language. Avoid attaching a filler to every sentence.
- Direction suggestions may ask for clear conversational pace, a brief pause before a contrast, or emphasis on one detail. Do not infer an exact cloned voice or perform exaggerated outrage.
